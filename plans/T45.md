# T45 · ✏️ Doodle type and the Sketchbook

Read `CLAUDE.md`, `plans/wave15.md` (shared rules: type id `doodle`, wool **244**, block ids **244-249**, tile prefix `DO_`),
`ROADMAP.md` "Wave 15" (the Doodle bullet), `STORY.md` "The Picture Worlds" and "Chapter 3", `plans/T40.md` (the Picture
World base), `plans/T38d.md` (the Swirl's tint: the model for a realm-only switch in the shared shader) and `plans/T41.md`
(the skill-game style) first.

**What the child does:** steps through the `secret1` frame (now **Sketchbook ✏️**) into a world that is a pencil drawing:
the same blocks and cubes as everywhere, but drawn in charcoal on warm paper, with wobbly lines that jump a little like a
cartoon, and the far edges of the world still blank paper ("not finished yet"). The pages of the sketchbook float apart;
the way between them is only a **dotted line**. Stand on a pencil pad, press **Draw!**, and a giant pencil draws the line
for you **while you keep your eye on its tip**. The line turns into a real bridge you walk on. Later the lines curl round
a tower, and on the last page **Rubber the Eraser** chases the pencil and rubs out the line behind it: trace fast and
steady to win the race. At the top waits **Artist Arlo** on his desk, the Boss with the Sketchbook star.

**Doodle is friendly and not the Scribble.** The Scribble (chapter 3) is messy, cold grey and grumpy. Doodle is the
opposite: warm cream paper, soft pencil, careful lines, smiling creatures that are *drawn on purpose but not finished
yet*. One bright colour is allowed: every Doodle creature wears a little **yellow crayon star** (its `gear`), so the
world never feels sad, and a child can tell the two apart at once.

## 0. The skill game: Trace! (and how it differs)

| Game | What your body does | What you judge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Football | run into / kick a rolling ball | power (the ring) |
| Basketball | jump and throw | the top of the jump |
| Race | run a track against a rival | speed and the racing line |
| Rowboats | stand still and aim, fire once | where a moving boat will be |
| Glowfish (T41) | swim in 3D, lunge | distance to a fleeing fish |
| **Trace! (T45)** | **follow a moving pencil tip with your eyes for 5-15 s at a time** | **a smooth, steady aim (and, on the last page, staying steady under pressure from the eraser)** |

- **No hold and let go** (used three times already) and **no single timing**: the skill is *continuous tracking*, the
  thing a pencil-and-paper "follow the dots" game trains. One tap of **Draw!** starts it; the rest is the view
  (mouse, or drag-to-look on the iPad).
- **Never a fail:** when your aim leaves the tip, the pencil just **waits** (it never goes back, except on page 3, and
  then only to the last star dot). A line is always finished in the end.
- **Three pages, three tricks** (each a bit harder):
  1. **Sketch Meadow: straight bridges** (3 lines). Slow tip, big ring. Learn it.
  2. **Curly Tower: curly lines** (2 lines): a zigzag-and-loop line, then a **spiral stair** that curls round and up a tall
     tower (the tip turns corners and climbs; the drawn stair is how you get up).
  3. **Eraser Hill: the race** (3 lines). Rubber the Eraser starts behind the pencil and rubs the line out. While your aim
     stays on the tip the pencil **speeds up** (a streak); if Rubber catches the tip, the line is rubbed back to the last
     **star dot** (a checkpoint every 8 blocks) and Rubber giggles and goes back to the start. Steady aim always wins.
- **Neat lines:** a line drawn with the aim on the tip at least 80% of the time says "Neat line! ✏️" and gives 1 bonus cube
  (once per line per trip). Messy lines still work: nothing is ever taken away.

## 1. The look (the heart of this task)

Five pieces, all behind switches that are **off everywhere but the Sketchbook**. Main world, Dream and Swirl must stay
**bit for bit** as they are (spec section C proves it in the same run).

### 1.1 The sketch atlas (a second texture, made once)

- New file **`src/sketchatlas.js`**: `drawSketchAtlas(atlas)` -> a canvas of **exactly the same size and grid** as the colour
  atlas (`ATLAS_COLS` x `ATLAS_ROWS`, tiles found only with `tileX` / `tileY`, never by hand), so every block's tile numbers,
  every uv in every chunk and the Swirl tint's tile ranges stay valid. Only the pixels differ.
- **How it is made (per tile, ImageData, not per-pixel `fillRect`; ~2-4 ms for ~360 tiles):**
  - Read the colour tile once (one `getImageData` of the whole colour atlas). Its alpha is copied 1:1 (glass, leaves and
    flower holes stay holes, so `alphaTest` still cuts them).
  - **Paper base:** `PAPER = [246, 242, 232]` (warm cream), washed a little darker for dark blocks
    (`paper * (1 - 0.16 * (1 - meanLuma))`, never below 80% of paper: the world stays light).
  - **Graphite strokes:** `INK = [62, 60, 70]` (soft blue-grey pencil, never pure black), each stroke pixel ±15% grain,
    about 1 in 12 stroke pixels left out (gappy, hand-drawn).
  - **The pattern is what tells kinds apart** (the owner's rule: not only shade). An explicit painter per tile name for
    every block the Sketchbook is built from (`SKETCH` table, below); every other tile gets the **auto pattern**: the
    colour tile's own detail (a Sobel edge map of its luma, threshold 0.12) drawn as strokes, plus density hatching for dark
    tiles. So bricks show brick lines, planks show boards, ores show dots, a player's odd block brought along still reads.
  - Own `mulberry32(4545)`: the same atlas on every device.

| Tile(s) | Pattern (16 x 16) |
| --- | --- |
| `GRASS_TOP` | short V-shaped grass flicks in loose rows (5-6 per tile) |
| `GRASS_SIDE` | paper with a scalloped flick band on the top 4 rows, two thin vertical crumbs below |
| `DIRT` | stipple: 22 single dots, 4 double dots |

- **Swapping, not redrawing** (chosen): `game.sketch` makes `sketchTexture = new THREE.CanvasTexture(drawSketchAtlas(atlas))`
  **lazily on the first Sketchbook visit** (same `NearestFilter`, no mipmaps, `SRGBColorSpace` as the colour texture) and
  keeps it for the session. Entering the Sketchbook sets `materials.solid.map = materials.water.map = materials.glow.map =
  sketchTexture`; leaving sets them back to `texture`. Same map presence and colour space = **no shader recompile** (spec
  checks `renderer.info.programs.length` before / after). Redrawing the one atlas in place was rejected: two full uploads
  per trip, the colour atlas would have to be redrawn (tens of ms) and the fire / portal tile uploads assume `texture`.
- **Memory:** today 512 x 112 px = 229 KB of RGBA once on the CPU (canvas) and once on the GPU; at the 1024-tile maximum
  512 x 512 = 1 MB each. Under 2 MB in the worst case, only after a Sketchbook visit.
- While the sketch map is on, `main.js` skips the Fireplace / Portal tile uploads (`!game.sketch.on`): the sketch versions of
  those tiles are still drawings, and the colour texture is not on screen.
- The hotbar and Blocks panel icons keep using the colour atlas (UI stays in colour).

### 1.2 Outlines and hatching in the shared shader (`caves.js`, one switch next to `swirlTint`)

```js
// T45: the Sketchbook's pencil look. uSketchOn is 0 everywhere but the Sketchbook, where the output is exactly what it was before.
export const sketchLook = {
  uSketchOn: { value: 0 },
  uSketchStep: { value: 0 },               // the boil: a whole number, 4 steps a second (game.sketch.update)
  uPaper: { value: new THREE.Color() },    // set from look.sketch.paper (sRGB hex -> linear like swirlsky's srgb)
  uInk: { value: new THREE.Color() },
  uSketchEdge: { value: new THREE.Vector4(0, 0, 1e9, 1e9) }, // x, z of the drawing's middle, r0, r1: the unfinished edge
};
```
- `patchMaterial(mat, { sketch = true } = {})` assigns `sketchLook` with `caveLight` and `swirlTint`; cache key
  `'cave-light-4'`. `{ sketch: false }` is **for the spec only** (the A/B check in C).
- **Vertex:** `attribute float sketchEdge; varying float vEdge; vEdge = sketchEdge;` (the face's edge mask, 1.3). A chunk
  outside the Sketchbook has no such attribute: WebGL then reads the default 0 (the builder checks there is no warning and
  no extra program; fallback if three.js complains: give the attribute only through `defines` in a second program key,
  never add it to main-world chunks).
- **Fragment**, right after `diffuseColor.rgb *= vTint;`, only `if (uSketchOn > 0.5)`:
  - `f = fract(vec2(vMapUv.x * uAtlas.x, (1.0 - vMapUv.y) * uAtlas.y))`: the position on the block face (every face is one
    quad with its whole tile, `World.addFace`). `uAtlas` is declared in the fragment shader too (same uniform object).
  - **Outlines:** for each of the 4 edges whose bit is set in `vEdge`: distance to that edge in pixels via `fwidth(f)`
    (constant 1.6 px lines at any distance), plus a **wobble** `0.035 * sin(along * 9.0 + h * 6.283)` (tile units) where
    `h = hash(cell, edge, uSketchStep)`, and **gaps**: `hash(cell, edge, floor(along * 3.0)) > 0.82` leaves a piece out
    (gaps are keyed to the cell only, not the step, so they never flicker). Ink strength 0.85, faded with distance
    (`smoothstep(30.0, 60.0, distance(vCavePos, cameraPosition))` takes it to 0.3) so far blocks do not turn into grey moiré.
  - **Hatching where it is darker:** `S` = the light factor already computed for `diffuseColor` (kept in a local). Below 0.8
    one set of diagonal strokes along `f.x + f.y` (7 per tile), below 0.55 a second set across (`f.x - f.y`), below 0.35 a
    third, each offset by `0.02 * hash(uSketchStep)` (it boils with the lines). Strokes are mixed toward `uInk` at
    `0.45 * (0.8 - S) / 0.8`. The sketch atlas's own patterns never use the `f.x + f.y` direction, so shade and kind never mix
    up.
  - **Unfinished edge:** `k = smoothstep(r0, r1, distance(vCavePos.xz, edge.xy))`: the fill goes to `uPaper` (`mix(..., 0.9 k)`),
    outlines only to half strength. The drawing fades into blank paper at the map's rim; the paper fog does the rest.
- **The glow material** (lamps, the way back, frames) gets the same fragment part through `patchSketch(materials.glow)` (only
  the sketch code, cache key `'sketch-glow-1'`; with `uSketchOn = 0` its output is unchanged: spec C).
- Cost: a few `fract`/`fwidth`/`smoothstep` per pixel, only in the Sketchbook; no extra pass, no extra draw call.

### 1.3 The edge mask (`world.js`, only in a sketch world)

Outlining every block edge would turn every field into graph paper. A real drawing has lines where something changes, so
`buildChunk` gives each face a 4-bit mask (bit 0 / 1: the `u = 0` / `u = 1` edge, bit 2 / 3: `v = 0` / `v = 1`; the world
direction of each edge comes from `face.corners`, worked out once into `EDGE_DIRS[face]`):
- an edge is drawn when the neighbour across it (in the face's plane) is **another block id**, or its own same face is
  **hidden** (`isOccluder(get(n + face.dir))`: a step or a corner), or it is air. Inside a flat grass field: no lines; at a
  cliff top, a step, a path of planks through grass, a tree trunk: lines.
- Only when `this.realm?.look?.sketch` (`this.sketch` flag set in the constructor): `addFace` pushes the mask four times
  into `buf.edge` and the geometry gets `sketchEdge`. Every other world takes the old path, attribute-free (+4 bytes per
  vertex only in the Sketchbook).
- The spec renders single blocks on paper and checks that the line sits on the right edge (a flipped `v` shows at once).

### 1.4 Paper sky and unfinished edges (`REALMS.doodle.look`)

`look: { sky: 0xf6f2e8, fog: [18, 66], time: null, weather: false, light: 1, still: true, stars: false,
sketch: { paper: 0xf6f2e8, ink: 0x3e3c46, edge: [80, 80, 64, 78] } }`. `updateSky`'s existing `look.still` branch already
paints the background and fog in `look.sky` and uses `look.fog` and `look.light`: **no `updateSky` change**. The close fog also
lets `World.cull` drop far chunks (fewer draw calls than the Swirl). Weather: `still` = always `clear` (T38d's `kindAt` line).

### 1.5 Boiling lines

`game.sketch.update(dt, now)` (called after `game.swirlSky.update` in `frame()`): `sketchLook.uSketchStep.value =
Math.floor(now / 250) % 64` while on. Gentle: only the wobble phase and the hatch offset change (2-3% of a block), gaps stay
put, 4 steps a second (hand-drawn animation "on twos" is 12; 4 is calm). Frozen while paused (the step is not advanced when
`!game.playing()` and not in a battle, so a screenshot is steady; `game.sketch.hold = true` for tests).

### 1.6 Creatures, people and the Boss (also in battles)

New file **`src/sketch.js`**: `createSketch(game, { materials, texture, atlas })` -> `game.sketch`
(`on`, `reset(world)`, `update(dt, now)`, `hold`, `info()`), and the module function **`sketchModel(group)`** (a no-op unless a
sketch world is loaded; reads a module flag set by `reset`).
- `sketchModel` walks the group and **patches each mesh's own material in place** (`onBeforeCompile` + cache key
  `'sketch-model-box'` for `BoxGeometry` meshes, `'sketch-model'` for others, `needsUpdate`). It never swaps materials,
  because `tintModel` (hit flashes, emissive), the rainbow colours and the Ninja / Squad code hold references to them.
  Skips `Line`, `Points`, sprites and meshes with `userData.keepColour` (the crayon star).
- Model fragment (after `#include <opaque_fragment>`): `g = luma(outgoingLight)`, colour = `mix(uInk, uPaper, smoothstep(0.08, 0.9, g))`
  (grey shading from the real lights, emissive flashes still show as a bright flash), screen-space hatching where `g < 0.55`
  (`fract((gl_FragCoord.x + gl_FragCoord.y) / 6.0 + step * 0.3)`, it boils), and for boxes **outlines on the box edges** from
  `vUv` with `fwidth` (every `BoxGeometry` face has uv 0..1). The uniforms are `sketchLook`'s (shared object).
- Hooks, one line each, `sketchModel(model)` right after the model is made: `creatures.js` (spawn), `npc.js` `Npc`
  constructor (people, Bosses, crew, other players' avatars), `battle.js` (ally, friend and foe models: four places),
  `riding.js` (the ride model). Not `ui.js` portraits (the team screen stays in colour).
- Models are rebuilt for every world and every battle and disposed as now, so nothing patched ever reaches another place.
  Riding stops on every trip (it does today), so the ride model never leaves the Sketchbook.
- Battle effects (`fx.js`) stay in colour: "coloured-pencil magic" (open question 2).
- The way-back `DoorGlow` stays coloured (the way out is easy to find), the guide and the HUD are UI.

### 1.7 Rejected: a full-screen post-processing pass

Edge detection + greyscale over the whole frame: an extra full-screen pass (heavy on iPads and in software WebGL), and
less control (no per-kind patterns, no unfinished edges, outlines on every depth step including creatures' faces). Kept
rejected, as the ROADMAP says.

## 3. Trace! (`src/tracing.js`, pure, no imports, Node-runnable)

```js
export const TRACE = {
  straight: { speed: 1.6, ring: 1.5 },          // blocks / s, ring radius (blocks, at the tip)
  curl:     { speed: 2.0, ring: 1.25 },
  race:     { speed: 2.4, top: 3.4, gain: 0.25, ring: 1.15, eraser: [2.0, 2.2, 2.4], eraserWin: 0.1, eraserCap: 2.8,
              eraserDelay: 2.5, star: 8 },        // the streak: +0.25 b/s per second on the tip, up to 3.4; star dots every 8
  grace: 0.25,      // off the ring for less than this still counts as on (little hand wobbles)
  near: 1.5, far: 24, // the tip only counts when it is 1.5-24 blocks in front of the camera
  touch: 1.25,      // ring x1.25 on touch devices
  neat: 0.8,        // on-fraction for "Neat line!"
  wait: 3,          // after 3 s off: "Look at the pencil tip!" and the tip glows
};
export function lineOf(def)            // -> { pts, len, at(s) -> [x, y, z], cells: [{ x, y, z, s }], stars: [s, ...] }
export function aimDist(eye, dir, tip) // distance from the tip to the view ray, or Infinity outside near..far
export function traceStep(st, dt, { eye, dir, touch }, line, kind, wins = 0)
  // st = { s, on, offFor, onTime, time, speed, eraser, rubbed, done } -> the same object, advanced by dt (clamped to 0.1)
  // on = aimDist <= ring (x touch) or offFor < grace; on: s += speed dt (race: speed grows by gain dt up to top), else
  // speed back to base; race: eraser (starts eraserDelay s after the first ink) moves at eraser[k] + eraserWin x wins
  // (capped) along the inked part; eraser >= s - 1 -> s = the last star dot behind it, eraser = 0, rubbed++ ("Hee hee!").
  // done when s >= len. Never Math.random; dt-summed, so 60 and 20 steps a second give the same line (spec F).
```

## 4. `DoodleLive` = `LIVE.doodle` (`src/doodlelive.js`, a `world.parts` part)

- **Draw!**: `near(p)` -> `'Draw!'` on a pad whose line is not drawn (and the previous line is; pads of later lines say
  "Draw the line before this one first!"), `'Stop'` while drawing; `use(p)` starts / stops. Plain `near` / `use` (a tap or E),
  never `holds`. The crosshair line says "Keep your eyes on the pencil tip!".
- **Meshes** (made with the part, removed on world reset; 6 draw calls): the dotted guide of every undrawn line (one
  `InstancedMesh` of small flat dashes, inked dashes scaled to 0), the **pencil** (hexagon `CylinderGeometry` + cone nib +
  eraser cap, 2 meshes, tilted at the tip, colour yellow `keepColour` with a sketch outline: the only big colour in the world
  besides the crayon stars), the **ring** at the tip (a dashed circle that turns solid and a bit bigger while you are on,
  1 mesh), **Rubber the Eraser** (a pink-grey eraser block with a paper sleeve and two drawn eyes, 2 meshes; only on page 3).
  The pencil and Rubber get `sketchModel` too.
- **Ink:** as `s` passes a cell's `s`, that cell becomes `DO_INK` through `LiveCells` (never inside the player; waits until
  you step off). Race rub-outs set cells back to `AIR` with `LiveCells` (standing on a rubbed cell drops you to the page
  below: puff back, 2.4; your star dots stay). Nothing is an edit or saved as a block.
- **Saved progress:** a finished line is a `beaten` key saved at once: `pw:doodle:line1..8` (`lineKey(n)`). On `reset` every
  saved line is drawn at once. `lineState(game)` -> `{ drawn: [bool x 8], next, done }`.
- **Falling:** standing on `DO_PAPER` (the page below) for 0.5 s puffs you (the Ninja `puff`, sound `smokepuff`) to the pad
  of the line you are drawing, else the pad of the furthest drawn line ("Whoops! Back up onto your page.").

# Challenge rework (owner 2026-10-27)

**Why:** Trace! (keep your aim on a pencil tip that draws the bridge for you) was mostly *following*: the child decides
nothing, so it risks feeling passive and easy, like the race. The owner's rule (CLAUDE.md): the way to a Boss must be a fun,
unique skill challenge: aim, dexterity, timing and directional tactics.

## What the child does: Draw! your own bridge

Stand on a star pad and press **Draw!** (E, a tap of the big button). The giant pencil starts at your feet and **draws
where you look**: the view ray meets the line's own floor in front of the pencil and the pencil turns toward that point
(at most `DRAW.turn` 2.0 rad/s, so lines are smooth curves). The pencil is a little faster than walking but never more
than `leash` 5 blocks ahead of you, so **you walk on your own line while you draw it**: stop and the pencil stops (the second pass made the line sticky: you no
longer run off its end, see below). The line is 3 blocks wide and climbs (or goes down) one block every `climb` (2) blocks
toward the next page's height by itself (no extra control: heights stay kid-sized).

- **Ink:** the pencil has limited ink (`ink` blocks of line, an ink bar in the score chip). Yellow **star dots** float in
  the gaps: steer the line through one and the ink is full again, and the star **keeps your line safe** up to it (a
  checkpoint). Out of ink = 1.2 s later you puff back to your last star.
- **Ink blots** (black blobs) and the pages that are not the next one block the pencil: steer round them. The Curly Tower
  blocks it too: to climb its 17 rows you **draw a spiral round the tower yourself** (the stars sit on the way up).
- **Doodle bees** (drawn bees that came alive) fly slowly back and forth across the gap (a closed form, there and back in
  7-8 s); if one touches the pencil tip (1.3 blocks) it nibbles up to 3 blocks of your line away (never the bit you stand
  on). Timing: stop for a moment (the pencil goes on to 5 blocks ahead of you, then waits) and go when the bee has passed.
- **Wind** (two Eraser Hill pages) pushes the pencil sideways while it draws: aim into the wind.
- **Rubber the Eraser** (Eraser Hill, lines 6-8) starts at the pad 2.5 s after the first ink and comes along your line,
  rubbing it out (never before your last star). He is a bit slower than walking: a child who keeps moving on a good path
  wins; one who stops, wanders or gets stuck behind a blot is caught: "Hee hee!" and back to the star.
- **Never a fail:** falling off (or being caught, or running dry) puffs you back to your last star dot (or the pad), the
  line stays up to there and Rubber goes back there too. Stop (the big button again) ends the try, the line is rubbed out.
- A line drawn without any setback says **Neat line!** (+1 Capture Cube, once per line per trip).
- The finished line stays as you drew it for this trip; it is saved as the same `beaten` key as before
  (`pw:doodle:line1..8`, old saves keep counting) and a later visit draws the old neat bridge (`doodleLines()`), so the walk
  graph check is unchanged. Artist Arlo's gate is unchanged; a won battle clears lines 6-8 (the race is new each rematch).
- The **gaps are keeps** (`gap` boxes over every column not next to a page): no block bridges and no flying across.

## Why it is skill, and why it is unique

| Skill | Where |
| --- | --- |
| aim (steering a line with the view, smooth enough to walk on) | every line, the spiral |
| dexterity (walk + look at once, do not run past the pencil) | every line |
| timing | bees (stop, then go) |
| directional tactics (short path vs. the star for ink, which side of a blot, into the wind, a good spiral) | lines 2-8 |
| pressure | Rubber on lines 6-8 |

No other world does this: football / basketball are a held power, the rowboats are a held cannon aimed at a moving boat,
the race is running a fixed track, Atlantis is swimming and lunging at fish, the Funfair, the Portal House and the Room of
Doors have their own toys. Here **the child makes the path itself, in real time, and walks on it**: a "snake" game in 3D.

## Numbers (`COURSES` in `doodleland.js`, `DRAW` in `tracing.js`)

**Second pass (orchestrator, same day): longer, more demanding lines.** The pages moved onto a ring (r about 56 round the middle;
the Cover and the arrival stay): Cover (south), Sketch Meadow (south-east), Little House (east), Eraser Hill (north-east), Pond Page
(north), the Desk (north-west), the Curly Tower (west), Eraser Hill 2 (south-west). Lines 1-2 go one step round the ring, 3-4 two
steps, line 6 three steps and line 7 four steps straight across the middle, line 8 two steps. Every line must **land on a dotted
strip** of the next page's edge (`land`; anywhere else the page's side stops the pencil: "Land on the dotted strip!"), so some
routes go round a page. The tower is 21 rows high now (deck y 40): the spiral is two turns. The neat bridge of a saved line is the
good route (3 wide); the dotted way is shown only on lines 1-2, after that only the landing strip.

**Controls after a real-physics stand-in walker (`/tmp` script, then `__drawReal` in the spec) got stuck on the sharper routes:**
- **Look past the pencil to steer it; look at your feet to walk.** The pencil turns only toward a point where the view ray meets
  the line's floor further from you than the pencil (`aimPoint`); looking at the pencil, nearer (where you walk) or at the sky keeps
  it going straight. So a child can watch their step and the pencil does not wander.
- **The line is sticky while you draw** (`DoodleLive.guard`): walking off its side or its end stops you at the edge, sliding along it.
  Falls now come from Rubber and from bees nibbling under you; the skill is steering while you keep walking.
- **Steps up hop by themselves** (`stepUp`): walking into a one-block step of your own line jumps you onto it (no Jump button for
  every step on a tablet).

## How it is checked (spec `doodle` F, G, H; `tests/doodlecheck.mjs`)

Model players (`DrawBot`: feet on the drawn line at walking speed, the view toward the bot's point) play every line in Node and
in the page: **skilled** (the course's `route`, waits for a gap in the bees) wins every line with no setback at 60 and 20 frames
a second (times within 0.3 s; also with the bees elsewhere at the start, at most one nibble, none on line 4); **careless** (looks
straight at the next page) never wins any line cleanly (the landing strip, blots, the tower, bees); **rush** (the good route but
never waiting for a bee) gets nibbled; **without the stars** lines 5-8 are not won
cleanly; **stopping** for 5 s on lines 6-8 gets you caught (line 7: the bees nibble the waiting pencil dry first) and the line is
still drawn after; Rubber never catches a player who has not left the pad. In the page: line 1 drawn while walking on it with
real Player physics at 60 and 20 steps a second, a careless real-physics try at line 2 (the blot stops the pencil, you walk off the
end and puff back to the pad), a skilled one round it, your own spiral walked up to the deck, Rubber, the rest of the play loop as
before, and on the emulated iPad one finger on the joystick while another drags the view.

Typical times for a skilled player: about 8 s on lines 1-2, 15-21 s on 3-5, 20-21 s on Eraser Hill (plus waiting for bees),
see the table.

# Look v2 (owner 2026-10-27)

**Why:** "it's simply a black & white version of the normal game. I had a completely different, a lot more sketch-based,
hand-drawn look in mind." The first atlas painted every face full of a dense grey per-tile pattern, so the world read as
greyscale textures. Look v2 makes the faces paper and lets lines, hatching and a few doodled marks carry the drawing.

**What changed (still no post-processing pass, still off everywhere else):**
- **The pencil atlas is a data atlas** (`sketchatlas.js drawSketchAtlas`, same size and grid): R = the tile's kind x 8, G = the colour
  tile's tone, B = auto detail (Sobel edges, only for tiles without a kind), A = alpha 1:1. Uploaded as `NoColorSpace`.
- **Marks texture** (`drawSketchMarks`, 768 x 384, one 96 px cell per kind, alpha strokes drawn once with canvas paths, mipmapped,
  ~1.5 MB on the GPU with mips): grass tufts, dirt dots, a crack or a pebble on stone, round cobbles, sand stipple, gravel rings,
  water waves, bark lines, log rings, scribbly leaf loops, pine chevrons, planks (three boards a block), bricks (four rows, half
  offset), big stone bricks, glass / ice shine, daisies, a little sun on lamps, knit wool, the star pad, a pencil on its side, the
  binding's rings, ore lumps, sandstone layers, mud, crystal facets. `ROTATE_KINDS` are turned / mirrored per block by a cell hash,
  the rest only mirrored, so neighbours never look stamped. They fade out below ~24 px a block (no grey mush far away).
- **The block shader `skDraw`** (caves.js, behind the `SKETCH` define + `uSketchOn`): paper with a soft world-space grain;
  **outlines** on the edge-mask edges as hand strokes 1.7-3 blocks long whose world axis comes from the edge attribute's new bits
  4-7 (`EDGE_AXES` in world.js), so a stroke runs on across blocks; each stroke sits 0.01-0.06 inside its face with its own slant
  (so strokes cross and overshoot at corners), overlaps the next one at joins, lifts once in a third of the strokes, has a varying
  weight and boils; 1.5-4.2 px wide by the block's size on screen, faded to 45% past 34 blocks. **Hatching**: two layers of broken,
  tapered, slightly bent diagonal strokes in world units, how many by the darkness `D` (face light, overhang shadow, the tile's
  tone): tops of light blocks stay paper, x-sides get sparse strokes, z-sides more, undersides and shadow cross-hatch and press
  harder; the spacing doubles with distance (one level, the boil hides the step). `DO_INK` (a drawn line) is scribbled graphite,
  `DO_PAPER` has faint blue ruled lines (the one coloured-pencil touch besides the crayon stars and the yellow pencil).
- **The `SKETCH` define:** the pencil code is compiled only into the Sketchbook's program; `game.sketch.reset` adds / removes the
  define (one recompile per trip, three.js drops the program again after leaving). Before, every world's shader carried the
  pencil code behind a uniform branch; in software WebGL that alone cost the main world a lot.
- **Models** (`sketch.js`): paper fill, screen-space broken hatching by the part's own colour and its light, solid graphite only for
  small, very dark parts (eyes, mouths; big dark parts are cross-hatched), wobbly box outlines up to 3.4 px, silhouette lines on
  round parts (normal vs view direction).
- Rejected on the way: marks written in GLSL per kind (a 30-branch function: SwiftShader ran every branch, ~2x slower than the
  texture), a sin-hash value noise for the grain (8 hashes a pixel), three hatch layers with two blended distance levels.

**Spec `doodle` changes:** B checks the data atlas (one kind and tone per tile, every named tile's kind, wool, auto detail, tone
order) and the marks texture (a few strokes per kind, mostly empty); D renders six kinds as slabs and checks they share few of
their pencil marks and stay mostly paper; the slab outline check moved 8 blocks east (the old spot was half hidden by the
Cover's own blocks once the faces stopped being grey); a frame is waited for before the sky / fog check; J adds a battle shot.
